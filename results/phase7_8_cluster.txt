=== HydraRT Phase 7+8: TCP node runtime, coordinator, remote work stealing ===

Hardware: Apple M2, 8 logical cores. All nodes are separate processes on loopback,
so they share those same 8 cores — no extra compute is added by adding nodes.

Workload: 4000 portable SyntheticCompute tasks (200k iterations each).
Node 3 submits everything; nodes 1 and 2 start empty and can only obtain work by
stealing it over TCP. Timing is the submitting node's wall clock to wait_all().

Reading
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Remote stealing wins whenever the origin node cannot use the cluster's full
capacity by itself, which is the case in both A and B: its workers occupy only
2 or 4 of the machine's 8 cores, and stealing lets peer processes take up the
rest. The speedup is larger in A because the origin starts more starved.

The B 'on' figure is the more informative one: it lands close to the single-node
8-worker control, i.e. the cluster recovers most of what shared memory would give
for the same cores. The remaining gap is serialization plus a TCP round trip per
steal batch, which is the price of being able to span machines at all.

Run-to-run variance is high (M2 mixes 4 performance and 4 efficiency cores, and
12 threads contend for 8 of them), so medians over 5 reps are reported rather
than single runs.
